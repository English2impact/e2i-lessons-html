Why intermediate learners stall

You know the grammar. You just can't build it at speaking speed.

At intermediate level the problem isn't knowledge — it's assembly time. Every second clause, every tense choice, every "he said that…" is a small calculation. Add them up and the conversation has moved on.

Tense by guesswork

"I have worked here since…" or "I worked here…"? You know the rule, but choosing it live costs two seconds every time.

One idea per sentence

You say three short sentences where one would do, because joining them with which, if or although still feels risky.

Stuck in first person

Passing on what a client said, what was decided, or what probably happened — reported speech, passive, must have — is where you freeze.

The rule hasn't changed: one structure — four forms. Only the sentences got longer. Here is session 9:

[ + ] If we sign it off today, they will deliver on Friday. Statement
[ − ] Unless we sign it off today, they will not deliver on Friday. Negative
[ Y/N ? ] Will they deliver on Friday if we sign it off today? Yes / no question
[ Wh- ? ] What will happen if we do not sign it off today? Information question

Roadmap · 4 stages · 24 structures

Reflex 1 grew the sentence outward. Reflex 2 grows it inward and backward.

Two clauses per sentence, tense contrast and unreal time. Each stage closes one complete ability, and from session 7 on every roleplay recycles a structure from an earlier stage so old reflexes don't go cold.

Stage 1 · Time Control

Sessions 1–6
  • S1We rolled out the new system last quarter.Finished past
  • S2I was going over the figures when the client called.Interrupted past
  • S3She has dealt with that supplier since March.Present perfect vs past simple
  • S4They have been looking into the delay all week.How long so far
  • S5By the time we called, they had already gone ahead with it.Past before past
  • S6I will be presenting the figures at ten.Fixed future

You canplace any action in past, perfect or future time without translating from Vietnamese.

Stage 2 · Clause Control

Sessions 7–12
  • S7The supplier that quoted us has raised the price.Defining relative clause
  • S8Our main client, whose office is in Hanoi, wants a call today.Non-defining relative clause
  • S9If we sign it off today, they will deliver on Friday.Real condition
  • S10If we had more budget, we would bring in a consultant.Unreal present
  • S11If we had double-checked the spec, we would have caught the error.Unreal past
  • S12I wish we had looped in the legal team earlier.Regret — wish / if only

You canadd a clause to a sentence: identify a thing, set a condition, and talk about what did not happen.

Stage 3 · Voice & Report

Sessions 13–18
  • S13The report was approved yesterday.Passive voice
  • S14The invoice must be settled this week.Modal & perfect passive
  • S15He said they were reviewing the contract.Reported statements
  • S16She asked whether we had sent the quote.Reported questions & instructions
  • S17They must be waiting for approval.Deduction about the present
  • S18They must have missed the email.Deduction about the past

You canreport statements, questions and instructions second-hand, and deduce with modals.

Stage 4 · Fluency Polish

Sessions 19–24
  • S19We agreed to postpone the launch.Verb patterns — to + V / V-ing
  • S20We had the system upgraded last month.Causative — have / get something done
  • S21This option is far more cost-effective than the other one.Comparison with degree
  • S22We moved the deadline so that the team could finish testing.Purpose & result
  • S23Although the budget was tight, we still delivered on time.Contrast
  • S24What we need is a clearer brief, wouldn't you say?Emphasis & hedging

You canchoose verb patterns, compare options, signal purpose and contrast, emphasise and hedge.

Quick questions

Before you start

Do I need to finish Reflex 1 first?

No, but session 1 assumes the present-tense core is already automatic. If "He gives it to him" or "I have given it to him" still needs thinking time, start with English Reflex 1 — or at least review its sessions 12–13 before session 1 here.

Is the workplace English industry-specific?

No. The workplace chunks are general professional English — meetings, email, deadlines, clients, reporting — the language used in any office job.

Why is this page in English?

From intermediate level, English2impact courses run fully in English. Vietnamese meanings are kept on the vocabulary flashcards.

What opens when I click the button?

The button opens English Reflex 2 in the app — no account needed. Session 1 is free for everyone. To keep your progress, sign in and press Add so the course sits in My learning; sessions 2–24 open with Pro.

I'm a teacher. Can I assign this course to my class?

Yes. Press Add to put the course in My teaching, then assign the 24 sessions as homework — audio, four-form drills and dialogues are ready-made, and you can follow each student's progress.

What can I do after 24 sessions?

Build long, accurate sentences at speaking speed: choose the right tense without translating, join ideas with clauses, report what others said, reason about what probably happened, and sound diplomatic when it matters.
